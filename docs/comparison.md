# CsHub: Comparison with Existing Solutions

## 1. Facebook groups

### What I observed

“I searched the group for a teacher’s name and got posts that were not related. Posts about exams, transport, and projects were mixed in the same feed. Several students asked the same question about Master options within a few weeks.”

### 3 strengths
1. Most students already have an account, so there is nothing new to join or learn.
2. Posting is fast and easy, including photos and screenshots, and replies often come quickly.
3. Students get notifications on their phones, which keeps the group active.

### 3 weaknesses
1. Posts about every topic (exams, transport, projects, teachers) are mixed in one feed, with no organization by subject.
2. Searching for an old question is unreliable, and old answers are buried under newer posts and lost over time.
3. Anyone can ask to join, so there is no guarantee that members are real students of the department. Students without Facebook are also left out.

### 3 strengths
1. Each community (subreddit) is dedicated to one topic, so content is organized.
2. Upvotes push the most useful answers to the top, and posts can be sorted by new or top.
3. Old discussions stay available and are indexed by search engines, so answers remain useful for years.

### 3 weaknesses
1. It is an international platform, mostly in English, so it has little content about our department, our teachers, or Algerian universities.
2. Anyone in the world can post, so there is no way to verify that members are students of our department.
3. Few of our students use it daily, so questions may get no answers, and joining a new platform adds effort.

## 3. Comparison table

| Criterion | Facebook groups | Reddit | CsHub |
|---|---|---|---|
| Content organized by topic | No (one mixed feed) | Yes (one subreddit per topic) | Yes (4 fixed communities) |
| Searching old answers | Weak | Good | Yes (keyword search in posts) |
| Focused on our department | Partly (if the group is ours) | No | Yes |
| Members verified as department students | No | No | Yes (admin approves accounts) |
| Arabic, French, and English content | Yes | Mostly English | Yes |
| Good answers rise to the top | No | Yes (votes) | Partly (likes, no downvotes) |
| Reporting bad content | Yes | Yes | Yes (report button, admin review) |
| Students need no social-media account | No (Facebook account needed) | No (Reddit account needed) | Yes (own simple account) |
| Control over the data | No (owned by Meta) | No (owned by Reddit) | Yes (our own database) |
| Already has many users | Yes | Yes | No (starts empty) |
| Ease of joining for students | Very easy | Medium | Needs admin approval |
## 4. Conclusion

Facebook groups are easy to join and widely used, but their posts are mixed together, old answers are hard to find, and nothing proves that members study in our department. Reddit organizes content better and rewards useful answers, but it is international, mostly in English, and has almost no content about our teachers, modules, and projects. Neither platform keeps the experience of each cohort organized and searchable for the next one.

CsHub fills this gap. It groups questions into fixed communities, lets students search old posts, shows useful answers through likes, and accepts only students approved by the admin. Its main limitation is that it starts with no users, so its value depends on the students of the department adopting it.
